# Module reflection

# Jakob Löhrer - Creative Coding

For the core of the module, we quickly grew bored of static image conversion and wanted our final piece to be dynamic and visual. After evaluating our resources, we pivoted to video, centering our concept around footage of sheep. From there, our ideas organically expanded. Having a foundational plan was crucial; because Bastian and I were relatively new to p5.js, that initial structure kept us grounded and prevented us from getting lost in the endless possibilities, even when we eventually deviated from the original blueprint.

Our main hurdle was performance. Live video conversion is incredibly heavy. My initial solution was simply to downscale the video resolution to reduce the pixel calculation load, but it wasn’t enough. During our first presentation in class, the computer overloaded and crashed. However, instead of seeing that as a failure, we treated it as an essential stress test, a necessary step to ensure our setup is bulletproof for tomorrow’s final presentation in the cinema.

**What did I try that worked well for me?**
Approaching visual design through code opened a massive new door for me. While I have significant JavaScript experience, I’ve never been able to draw by hand. Treating code as a canvas allowed me to generate artistic visuals I could never have sketched traditionally. Because I already understood the core programming logic, the technical side felt relaxed. I wasn’t constantly struggling with syntax, which gave me the bandwidth to actually enjoy the creative process and help my classmates debug their projects, which was highly rewarding. Furthermore, the Colabor structure itself was refreshing; breaking out of my usual cohort to work alongside students from different disciplines brought entirely new viewpoints to our workflow.

**What would I do differently next time?**
Moving forward, my primary focus would be figuring out how to bridge the gap between experimental creative coding and my main interest: web development. Right now, I am uncertain how perfectly these heavy, performative canvases translate into standard web environments. Next time, I would try to explicitly merge these domains, using generative visual coding not just as standalone performance art, but as an integrated extension of my front-end web development skills.
